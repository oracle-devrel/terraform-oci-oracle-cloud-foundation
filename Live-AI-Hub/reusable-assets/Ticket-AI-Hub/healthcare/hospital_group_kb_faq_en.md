## 4. Emergency and Walk-In Consultations

### KB-URG-001 — Manchester Triage System

All Hospital Group emergency departments use the **Manchester Triage System**, which classifies patients by clinical severity using coloured wristbands:

| Wristband | Priority | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 Red | Immediate | Immediate life-threatening risk |
| 🟠 Orange | Very Urgent | Risk situation within 10 minutes |
| 🟡 Yellow | Urgent | Urgent situation, within 60 minutes |
| 🟢 Green | Less Urgent | Non-urgent situation, within 120 minutes |
| ⚪ Blue | Non-Urgent | Very low urgency situation |

Waiting time depends on the clinical priority assigned, not the order of arrival.

### KB-URG-002 — Adult Emergency

The adult emergency service is available **24 hours a day, 7 days a week** at all main hospital units.

### KB-URG-003 — Paediatric Emergency

Urgent paediatric care is available **every day from 08:00 to 22:00**. Outside these hours, urgent situations should be directed to the adult emergency department or INEM (112).

### KB-URG-004 — Walk-In Consultation

The Adult Walk-In Consultation is intended for non-serious acute illness that does not warrant hospital emergency attendance. It operates **every day from 08:00 to 22:00**.

**Not appropriate for:**
- Life-threatening situations (call 112)
- Management of chronic conditions (book a specialist appointment)
- Prescription renewals (contact your GP)

### KB-URG-005 — Health Line

The **Health Line 21 770 41 16** is a free telephone clinical assessment service conducted by qualified nurses, available **every day from 08:00 to 24:00**. After assessment, the patient is directed to:
- Emergency or walk-in consultation
- SOS Teleconsultation
- Scheduled specialist appointment
- Self-care guidance at home

> ⚠️ This line **does not replace 112** in emergency situations.

---

### FAQ — Emergency

**What is the difference between emergency and walk-in consultation?**
Emergency care is for potentially serious situations requiring immediate assessment and treatment. The walk-in consultation is for minor acute illness (e.g. fever, flu, urinary infection) that is not urgent.

**Do I have to pay at the emergency department?**
Yes. Urgent care involves payment of an emergency fee, unless exempt due to health scheme, health insurance or social circumstances. Confirm your insurance conditions before attending.

**How long will I wait in the emergency department?**
Waiting time depends on clinical priority (Manchester triage) and patient volume. Fixed waiting times cannot be guaranteed. The Health Line can help you decide whether to attend the emergency department.

**Does the paediatric emergency see newborns?**
Yes, within operating hours (08:00–22:00). For serious situations outside these hours, go to the adult emergency department or call 112.

## 5. Inpatient Admission

### KB-INT-001 — Admission to Hospital

Admission may be planned (elective surgery, treatment) or urgent (via the emergency department). In the case of planned admission, you will receive in advance:
- Admission date and time
- Preparation instructions (fasting, medications to suspend, etc.)
- List of documents and personal items to bring

### KB-INT-002 — What to Bring for Admission

- Identity document and citizen card
- Health insurance or health scheme card
- Regular medication (in original packaging with leaflet)
- Personal hygiene items
- Comfortable clothing
- Mobile phone charger (the hospital provides free Wi-Fi)
- Do not bring valuables

### KB-INT-003 — Visiting Inpatients

Visits are permitted **every day from 15:00 to 19:00**. Each patient may receive up to **2 visitors at a time**. In special situations (ICU patients, children, palliative care), visiting rules are defined by the clinical team.

### KB-INT-004 — Meals and Accommodation

Inpatients receive meals included in the admission (breakfast, lunch, dinner and supper). A companion may stay in private rooms for an additional daily charge. Contact the unit administration for rates.

### KB-INT-005 — Hospital Discharge

Discharge is authorised by the responsible doctor. At discharge, the patient receives:
- Medical discharge report
- Prescription for medication
- Follow-up instructions and next appointment
- Information on home care

---

### FAQ — Inpatient Admission

**Can I have a companion during admission?**
In private rooms it is possible to have a companion 24h by prior arrangement and payment of a daily charge. In wards, companions follow the general visiting rules.

**Does the hospital have free Wi-Fi?**
Yes, all inpatient units provide free Wi-Fi for patients and companions.

**When will I receive the invoice for my admission?**
The invoice is issued at discharge or sent by email in the following days. In the case of insurance, part of the process may be managed directly between the hospital and the insurer.

3. The hospital validates the coverage directly with the insurer

If you paid the full consultation cost and wish to claim reimbursement from your insurer:
1. Keep the original receipt itemising the procedures performed

### KB-SEG-005 — Prior Authorisation for Surgery and Admission

Some insurers require **prior authorisation** for planned admissions, surgery and high-cost imaging exams. This authorisation must be obtained **before** the procedure. Hospital Group can assist with this process — contact our admissions team in advance.

### KB-ESP-003 — GP vs. Specialist

The GP (General Practice) is the ideal entry point for most health situations. They can:
- Treat simple acute problems
- Manage chronic conditions
- Issue referrals to specialists when necessary
- Renew regular medication

Consult a specialist directly when:
- You have a specific already-diagnosed problem
- You want a second opinion
- Your GP is unavailable and the situation is not urgent

### KB-ESP-004 — Psychology vs. Psychiatry Consultation

| Prescribing medication | ❌ | ✅ |

### FAQ — Specialties

**I don't know which specialty I should see. What should I do?**
Call the Health Line 21 770 41 16 (08:00–24:00, every day). Qualified nurses will guide you free of charge to the most appropriate channel.

**Do I need a referral to see a specialist?**
Under private or health insurance arrangements, a referral is not required for most specialties. Under NHS (SNS) agreement, a referral letter from the GP is required.

**What is the difference between a psychologist and a psychiatrist?**
A psychiatrist is a medical doctor and can prescribe medication. A psychologist provides therapy but cannot prescribe. In cases of severe anxiety or depression, a psychiatric assessment is recommended. Having support from both can be beneficial.

## 12. Patient Rights and Responsibilities

### KB-DIR-001 — Patient Rights

In accordance with Law No. 15/2014 and other applicable legislation, the patient has the right to:

- **Access to healthcare** within the guaranteed maximum response times
- **Clear information** about their health status, diagnosis, treatments and risks
- **Informed consent** — no treatment may be performed without their consent (except in emergencies)
- **Confidentiality** of clinical data and respect for privacy
- **Second medical opinion** without affecting the quality of care provided
- **Access to their own clinical record**
- **Submit complaints** and receive a response within the legal deadline (15 working days)
- **Be treated with dignity and respect** regardless of social status, ethnicity, religion or gender

### KB-DIR-002 — Patient Responsibilities

The patient has a duty to:
- Provide truthful and complete information about their health status
- Respect the hospital's operating rules
- Attend scheduled appointments or cancel with adequate notice
- Pay the charges due for the care received
- Treat all healthcare professionals and other patients with respect
- Comply with prescribed recommendations and treatments

### KB-DIR-003 — Informed Consent

Before any procedure, surgery or invasive examination, the patient will be asked to sign an **informed consent form**. This document confirms that the patient has been informed about:
- The procedure and its objectives
- The risks and benefits
- Available alternatives
- The right to refuse or withdraw consent at any time

You may complain through any channel — physical, online, email or in person. All carry the same legal weight.

**How long will it take to receive a response to my complaint?**
Within a maximum of 15 working days from submission. If you do not receive a response within that period, you may contact the ERS (Health Regulatory Authority) at www.ers.pt.

**Can I complain anonymously?**
Yes, you may submit an anonymous complaint, but this will limit the hospital's ability to provide you with a personalised response or to request additional clarification.
